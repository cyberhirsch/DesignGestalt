Gestalt playground

Five principles you can push until they break. Move each slider slowly and watch for the moment the grouping flips — that tipping point is the whole lesson, and it is much easier to feel than to be told.

Law of Proximity

Distance decides what belongs together

Identical dots, one variable. Widen the gaps and the field reads as rows; narrow them and it snaps into columns. Nothing about the dots themselves changed.

Law of Similarity

Likeness beats position

Spacing stays perfectly even here. Raise the difference and shape alone pulls the grid apart into groups — strongly enough to override the even distances.

Law of Closure

The eye finishes the shape

Open the circle and you keep seeing a circle long past the point where most of it is missing. Find where it finally stops being one — that threshold is further than anyone expects.

Law of Continuity

A smooth path wins over a broken one

Two lines cross. You read them as two continuous strokes passing through each other — never as four separate arms meeting at a point. Bend one and the reading holds until the path stops being smooth.

Law of Common Fate

Moving together beats standing near

The dots are identical and evenly spaced, so neither similarity nor proximity can group them. Raise the amplitude and two non-adjacent columns start moving as one — a group appears out of nothing but shared motion.

Law of Prägnanz

The simplest reading available wins

One shape, one fill, one outline. Slide the two forms apart and you read a circle behind a square; push them together and the simple reading disappears, leaving a single blob the eye cannot decompose.

Figure & Ground

Which half is the subject?

The classic ambiguous edge: two faces, or a vase. Shift the balance and one reading takes over. In the middle both compete — and that instability is exactly what a poster must never leave to chance.
